The Uganda Most Travellers Miss

Six quieter corners of the Pearl of Africa, and why they deserve a place on your next itinerary.

Sep 30, 2026 · @TJ

There is a version of Uganda that most visitors never see. Not because it is hidden, but because it sits just off the usual route: a lake the tour bus drives past, a waterfall on the far side of the country, an island a ferry ride away.

The last edition took you to Uganda's headline destinations. In this one, we are going somewhere quieter. These are the places our team talks about when someone asks where we would go with a free weekend, or a free week.

Some are a short drive from Kampala. One is about as remote as East Africa gets. All of them are worth the detour.

Southwestern Uganda

1. Lake Bunyonyi: The Lake That Asks Nothing of You

Some places are for doing. Lake Bunyonyi is for being. Its name is often translated as "place of many little birds", and it lives up to it: 29 small islands scattered across still water, ringed by hillsides terraced so neatly they look hand-drawn.

Days here have a simple rhythm. Paddle a dugout canoe between the islands. Walk the ridge paths above the water. Swim, read, nap, and repeat. In the evening, mist settles on the lake and the only sounds are birdsong and the occasional canoe gliding past below.

Bunyonyi also sits within easy reach of Bwindi's southern trekking sectors, which makes it the perfect place to rest before or after a gorilla trek.

Things to Do
  • Island-hop by dugout canoe with a local guide, hearing the stories behind islands such as Punishment Island
  • Take a boat tour across the lake to see its most scenic corners in a single morning
  • Swim in the calm, cool water straight from your lodge's jetty
  • Hike the ridges above the lake for sweeping views over the islands and terraced hills
  • Cycle or mountain bike along the quiet lakeshore roads and village trails
  • Go birdwatching: the lake lives up to its name, with more than 200 species recorded
  • Visit a local community to learn about farming life and traditional crafts around the lake

This is where you go when the itinerary needs a pause.

Eastern Uganda

2. Sipi Falls: Coffee, Cliffs and Cool Mountain Air

On the foothills of Mount Elgon, three waterfalls tumble down a green escarpment, one after another. The tallest drops almost 100 metres into the valley below, and on a clear day the view stretches far across the plains.

Sipi rewards the traveller who likes to walk. A guided hike links all three falls through farmland, small villages and forest paths. The brave can abseil beside the main falls. Everyone else can find a good viewpoint and simply watch the water.

Then there is the coffee. The slopes of Mount Elgon produce some of Uganda's finest Arabica, and a morning with a local farmer, from picking to roasting to that first cup, may well become the highlight of your trip. Pack a jumper: evenings up here are refreshingly cool.

Things to Do
  • Abseil almost 100 metres down the cliff face beside the main waterfall, the ultimate Sipi adrenaline rush
  • Hike to all three falls on a guided half-day or full-day walk through farms and villages
  • Join a coffee tour and follow Mount Elgon Arabica from bean to cup with a local farming family
  • Go rock climbing on the escarpment's routes, suitable for beginners and experienced climbers
  • Mountain bike the trails around Kapchorwa, with the falls and plains as your backdrop
  • Trek on Mount Elgon, from a day hike to a multi-day climb towards the caldera
  • Picnic at the base of the falls and look out for the area's birdlife

For the traveller who likes their views earned, and their coffee fresh.

Lake Victoria

3. The Ssese Islands: Island Time on Lake Victoria

Most people do not think of Uganda as a beach destination. The Ssese Islands are here to change that. Scattered across the northwestern corner of Lake Victoria, this archipelago of more than 80 islands offers sandy shores, tropical forest and a pace of life that slows the moment you step off the ferry.

Bugala, the largest island, is the easiest to reach, by ferry from Entebbe or via the shorter crossing near Masaka. Once there, the plan is refreshingly simple: long walks along the shore, cycling through the forest, fresh fish for lunch and a front-row seat to some of the most beautiful sunsets on the lake.

It is also one of the best escapes for families and groups of friends, close enough for a long weekend and far enough to feel properly away.

Things to Do
  • Relax on the beach at Lutoboka Bay on Bugala Island, the archipelago's best-known stretch of sand
  • Island-hop by boat to quieter, less-visited islands and fishing villages
  • Go sport fishing for Nile perch and tilapia with local fishermen
  • Walk or cycle through the islands' tropical forest and palm plantations
  • Kayak along the shoreline in the calm early-morning water
  • Take a sunset cruise out on Lake Victoria
  • Go birdwatching for hornbills, fish eagles and kingfishers

Sometimes the best holiday is simply an island, a book and nowhere to be.

Western Uganda

4. Lake Mburo: The Safari You Can Do in a Weekend

Not every safari needs a week off work. Lake Mburo is the closest savannah park to Kampala, and it may be the most underrated. Rolling grassland, acacia woodland and a string of lakes are home to zebras, impala, eland, buffalo, giraffes and plenty of hippos.

What makes Mburo special is how you get to explore it. With no elephants and very few lions, this is one of the few parks in Uganda where you can leave the vehicle behind. Take a guided walking safari at dawn, ride out among the zebras on horseback, or cycle through the park, then round off the day with a boat trip on the lake.

It is also a natural first stop for anyone heading to Bwindi or Lake Bunyonyi, breaking a long drive into something to look forward to.

Things to Do
  • Horseback safari among zebras, eland and impala, one of the few places in East Africa to do it
  • Walking safari at dawn with an armed ranger guide
  • Cycling safari on the park's trails
  • Game drives by day, plus night drives for leopards, hyenas and bushbabies
  • Boat trip on Lake Mburo to see hippos, crocodiles and, if you are lucky, the elusive African finfoot
  • Sport fishing at the park's designated fishing spot
  • Visit a Banyankole homestead to meet the famous long-horned Ankole cattle and learn about local pastoral life
  • Go birdwatching: more than 300 species have been recorded here

Leave Kampala on Friday. Be on safari by sundown.

Far Northeastern Uganda

5. Kidepo Valley: Africa With No One Else in the Frame

There is remote, and then there is Kidepo. Tucked into the far northeastern corner of the country, close to the borders with South Sudan and Kenya, Kidepo Valley National Park is Uganda's wildest and least-visited park. That is exactly its appeal.

The landscape feels different from anywhere else in Uganda: wide golden plains ringed by rugged mountains, with the Narus Valley at its heart. Lions rest on rocky outcrops. Large herds of buffalo move across the grassland. Ostriches, giraffes, elephants and, if you are lucky, cheetahs complete the picture. You can drive for hours without passing another vehicle.

The journey is part of the story. Charter flights from Entebbe take around two hours, while the road trip through Karamoja takes a full day and rewards you with a glimpse of Karamojong culture along the way.

Things to Do
  • Game drives in the Narus Valley, the park's wildlife heartland, for lions, buffalo, elephants and cheetahs
  • Guided nature walks with a ranger across the open savannah
  • Visit the Kanangorok Hot Springs in the Kidepo Valley, close to the South Sudan border
  • Hike in the Morungole Mountains and visit the Ik, one of Uganda's smallest and most remote communities
  • Spend time in a Karamojong manyatta to learn about the pastoral traditions of the region
  • Go birdwatching: Kidepo is home to species found nowhere else in Uganda, including the ostrich and Karamoja apalis
  • Enjoy a sundowner on a rocky outcrop as the plains turn gold

For the traveller who wants the wilderness all to themselves.

Central Uganda

6. Mabamba Wetland: A Morning with the Shoebill

Not every unforgettable encounter requires a long journey. Just west of Entebbe, on the edge of Lake Victoria, lies Mabamba Bay, a protected wetland of papyrus channels and quiet water. It is one of the best places in the world to find the shoebill.

With its giant bill, steady stare and almost prehistoric presence, the shoebill is one of Africa's most sought-after birds. Here you go looking for it the local way, gliding through the papyrus in a wooden canoe with a guide who knows exactly where to look. Along the way, kingfishers, jacanas and herons keep you company.

Start early, when the birds are most active and the light is soft, and you can be back in Entebbe in time for a long lakeside lunch.

Things to Do
  • Canoe through the papyrus channels in search of the shoebill with a local guide
  • Go birdwatching for hundreds of other species, from pygmy geese to malachite kingfishers
  • Visit a lakeside fishing village to see daily life on Lake Victoria
  • Take a boat trip to Ngamba Island Chimpanzee Sanctuary from Entebbe to see rescued chimpanzees
  • Stroll the Entebbe Botanical Gardens, home to monkeys and plenty more birdlife
  • Visit the Uganda Wildlife Conservation Education Centre in Entebbe, ideal for families
  • Finish with a long lunch at a lakeside restaurant in Entebbe

Proof that some of Uganda's most extraordinary moments are just an hour from home.

Ground Control

On the Road: A Uganda Road-Trip Checklist

Most of this week's destinations are best reached by road. Here is how to make the drive part of the pleasure.

01
Choose the right vehicle

A 4x4 is worth it for Kidepo, the hills around Bunyonyi and Sipi in the rainy season. For shorter trips on tarmac, a comfortable saloon car will do.

02
Consider a driver-guide

An experienced driver-guide knows the roads, the shortcuts and the best roadside stops, and doubles as your spotter on game drives. It also means you get to enjoy the view.

03
Drive in daylight

Plan your days so you arrive before dark. Roads are harder to read at night, and the scenery is half the reason you are driving.

04
Fuel up and carry cash

Fuel stations thin out in remote areas, especially in Karamoja. Fill up in the larger towns, and keep some Ugandan shillings for small shops, markets and local guides.

05
Sort park and activity bookings in advance

Park entry, boat trips, horseback safaris and guided walks are best arranged ahead of time. Check current payment methods with the Uganda Wildlife Authority or your operator before you set off.

06
Know when to fly, or float

For Kidepo, a charter flight turns a full day on the road into a two-hour hop. For the Ssese Islands, check the latest ferry times before you travel, as schedules can change.

So, Where Will You Go Next?

Perhaps you have driven past the turn-off to Lake Mburo a dozen times. Perhaps Sipi has been "next year" for the last five years. Perhaps you have never heard of Mabamba until today.

That is the thing about Uganda. Just when you think you know it, it shows you another lake, another valley, another quiet morning you did not know you needed.
